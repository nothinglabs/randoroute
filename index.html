Help

Routing settings

Scroll ↓

Presets

A preset replaces your active rules and route preferences. Use its Rules button to review the full setup. Adjusting any control switches to custom rules.

  • The Randonneur — widest choice of routes; looser rules and fewer roads flagged as failing.
  • Weekend Wanderer — slower roads with practical flexibility; concerns pulse red.
  • Casual Cruiser — low-stress riding; routes must fully follow your safety rules.

Limits

  • Minimum shoulder — the shoulder width required on faster roads.
  • Max speed without shoulder — roads at or below this speed can pass when shoulder data is missing.
  • Never allow roads faster than — faster ordinary roads fail; dedicated bike infrastructure is exempt.
  • No cutoff — disables the speed cap and leaves the shoulder rule in charge.

Options

  • Heavily prefer bike routes & trails — favors bike facilities and designated corridors; physical infrastructure gets the stronger benefit.
  • Prefer residential streets — every route choice favors streets mapped as residential or living streets.
  • Trust designated bike routes — lets a USBR or regional designation satisfy the shoulder rule. Speed limits and access restrictions still apply.
  • Allow freeway as last resort — a freeway is permitted only as a heavily penalized fallback, and it still fails the rules.
  • Allow mountain bike trails — makes explicitly MTB-tagged paths available, but strongly avoided. Any use appears in Route Details.
  • Only show routes fully matching safety rules — hides options with rule failures. Minor exception: a short access link near a pin may remain and appear red.
  • Unknown shoulder = 0 ft — if shoulder data is missing, treat it as 0 ft for rules purposes.

Search always includes local offline results; the ⌕ button also tries an online lookup.

Voice navigation

  • Off-route: Notify only — gives direction and distance back to your route.
  • Off-route: Route back to route — automatically routes to the nearest point on your route.
  • Off-route: Dynamic re-routing — creates a new route to your destination through remaining stops.
  • Speak compass directions (N/S/E/W) — adds headings to turn instructions.
  • Status update — choose how often it speaks, and whether it includes route status, current speed, miles remaining, and estimated time left.

Advanced routing weights

Usually leave these alone. Presets and Limits are the intended controls. Weights are soft preferences for testing and fine-tuning; unusual values can create long or unintuitive routes. They never override bicycle prohibitions or hard Limits, and Reset all defaults restores them.

    Help

    Map layers & data

    Scroll ↓

    Toggling layers only adjusts visibility. All data is always used for routing.

    Data sources

    • Designated routes (USBR & regional): mapped bicycle-route relationships from OpenStreetMap. They provide route context, not proof of a bike facility.
    • WSDOT BLTS (state highways): official state-road stress, shoulder and access context, legal speeds, and bike-facility data.
    • OSM bike infrastructure: mapped bike lanes, cycle tracks, shared-use paths, and other bicycle facilities. It distinguishes off-street paths from on-street facilities.
    • Bikes prohibited (WSDOT): permanent state restriction data. These graph segments are excluded from routing.
    • All roads (OpenStreetMap, estimated speeds): the connected road and path network, including names, direction, bicycle access, ferries, and residential streets. Missing legal speeds use an estimate.

    Elevation & cautions

    Elevation supplies climb, descent, grade, and travel-time estimates. The app also flags a possible limited-visibility uphill curve when road geometry, grade, speed, and facility data suggest extra care. It is a directional planning cue—not measured sight distance—and does not itself fail your rules.

    How choices are made

    The app tests several time, safety, and road-preference combinations, removes near-duplicates, and shows up to five meaningfully different routes. Freeways are a last resort, legal limited-access highways are cautions, and permanent bicycle restrictions are impassable. A physical bike facility gets a stronger routing benefit than designation alone.

    Technical data notes

    Missing speed limits use a road-class estimate, and the street popup identifies estimates. Roads tagged bicycle=no or bicycle=dismount are excluded. Explicit MTB paths are excluded by default and can be enabled with a strong penalty. Higher OSM road classes add soft costs when no bike facility is recorded; this is a traffic-volume proxy, not a rule failure.

    Limits of the data

    Coverage and tagging vary by place. Missing data is not proof that a shoulder, facility, traffic condition, surface, crossing control, or sight line is absent. Short crossings are inferred from route geometry and do not confirm a signal or crosswalk. Use local signs and conditions; this is a planning aid, not a guarantee of safety.
    Route help

    Route information

    Scroll ↓

    Choose Route

    • The app can show up to five route options, A–E, generated by different route searches.
    • Tap A–E to show distance, estimated time, elevation, and the route’s road and safety information.
    • Click Details to see in-depth information on the route.

    Details

    • Concerns: shows steps with rule failures, freeways, limited-access roads, mountain-bike trails, and uphill-curve cautions.
    • All Steps: every route segment in order.
    • Tap buttons in route segments to view them on the map or with Google Street View.

    Safety ratings & map colors

    • Lime — Bike network: a recorded bike facility (bike lane, etc.).
    • Dotted lime — off-street paths and shared-use trails.
    • Blue — road that passes rules (speed and shoulder requirements).
    • Red dashed — Road that fails rules. A known rule conflict, freeway, or permanent bicycle restriction. Failing lane segments pulse. Permanent bicycle restrictions are never routed.
    • Amber — Caution: a bike-legal limited-access highway or allowed mountain-bike trail. It deserves extra judgment.
    • Dashed light blue — Designated bike route: a signed or mapped bicycle corridor. This is an additional overlay; it does not indicate a safety level.
