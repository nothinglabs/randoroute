Routing settings

Limits

Minimum shoulder is the width required on faster roads. Max acceptable speed without shoulder lets slower roads pass even where shoulder data is missing. Never allow roads faster than makes roads above the selected speed fail; “no cutoff” leaves the shoulder rule in charge.

Presets

Each preset applies a complete baseline for riding rules and route preferences. Randonneur has no speed limit with an adequate shoulder and allows 35 mph without one. Weekend Wanderer allows 45 mph with an adequate shoulder and 25 mph without one. Casual Cruiser allows 35 mph with an adequate shoulder and 25 mph without one. Presets also set designated-route, freeway, and fully-matching-route behavior while leaving advanced weights and navigation settings unchanged.

Options

Strongly prefer bike routes for all route options forces every choice to favor bike facilities and designated corridors; physical infrastructure receives the stronger routing benefit. Strongly prefer residential streets for all route options forces every choice to favor streets mapped as residential or living streets. Treat designated bike routes as vetted lets a USBR or regional designation satisfy the shoulder rule; turn it off to evaluate those roads by the same speed and shoulder limits as every other road. Allow mountain bike trails makes OSM paths explicitly tagged for mountain biking available, but still strongly avoided; it is off by default and any use is called out in Route Details. Allow freeway as last resort permits a freeway only as a heavily penalized fallback; it still fails the rules. Only show routes fully matching safety rules limits every option to routes whose segments all match your active safety rules, and reports when none exists. Auto-reroute waits for moving time while you remain off route, then announces and updates the route. Unknown shoulder treated as 0 ft requires faster roads to prove an adequate shoulder.

Advanced routing weights

Most riders should not change these. Presets and Limits are the intended controls. Weights expose internal soft costs for testing and fine-tuning; poor combinations can produce long or unintuitive routes. They never override bicycle prohibitions or your hard Limits, and can be restored with Reset all defaults.

Help

Map data & routing graph

The app combines official Washington data with OpenStreetMap and elevation into one offline, on-device routing graph. Layer switches change what is drawn; they do not remove data from routing.

Official WSDOT data

Bicycle Level of Traffic Stress (BLTS) supplies statewide road stress, measured shoulder context, and access-control information. Roadway Characteristic Data supplies legal speeds on state highways. Active Transportation Facilities identifies shared lanes, bike lanes, buffered or separated lanes, and shared-use paths. Permanent bicycle restrictions are hard exclusions: the router never traverses them.

OpenStreetMap data

OSM supplies the connected rideable road and bicycle-path network, street names, signed speeds where tagged, road class, one-way rules, shoulders, bicycle access, cycleways, designated national/regional bicycle routes, ferries, and residential streets. Missing speed limits use a documented road-class estimate; the street popup says when speed is estimated. Ways tagged bicycle=no or bicycle=dismount are not part of the riding graph. Paths explicitly tagged mtb:*, or in an OSM route=mtb relation, are hidden and excluded by default; Settings can make them available with a strong penalty.

Elevation & curve cautions

AWS Terrarium elevation tiles provide climb/descent and grade-aware travel time. The builder also looks for a tight curve that overlaps an uphill grade on a faster road without a protective facility, then adjusts concern using known shoulder width. This is a possible limited-visibility uphill curve, directional and separately reported. It is a geometry/elevation proxy—not measured sight distance—and does not by itself fail your riding rules.

How routes are chosen

The router tests several time/safety and road-preference combinations, removes nearly identical paths, and presents up to five meaningfully different results. Rule failures, freeways, limited access, curve cautions, speed, grade, facilities, designated routes, and residential preference affect cost. A physical bike facility receives a stronger routing benefit than designation alone; if an edge has both, the facility benefit wins. OSM tertiary, secondary, primary, and trunk classes also add increasing soft costs when no bike facility is recorded; this is a traffic-volume proxy, not a rule failure. Freeways remain a last resort; legal limited-access highways are cautions; permanent prohibitions are impassable.

What each map layer means

  • Designated routes (USBR & regional): official national/regional OSM bicycle-route relationships, drawn as a dashed blue ribbon beneath the safety verdict. Designation is useful routing context, not proof of a bike facility. A lime facility, muted-purple caution, or red failure remains visible on top. Turning the layer off hides only the ribbon; the router still uses the route data.
  • WSDOT BLTS (state highways): official state-road stress and shoulder/access context.
  • OSM bike infrastructure: mapped bike lanes, cycle tracks, shared-use paths, and other bicycle facilities. Off-street bicycle paths and trails use a lime line with a fine dotted center; on-street accommodations are solid. Explicit mountain-bike paths are hidden unless enabled in Settings.
  • Bikes prohibited (WSDOT): permanent state restriction data for inspection; these graph segments are excluded from routing.
  • All roads (OSM, est. speeds): the broader street network, including residential roads; its visibility is reduced when zoomed out to keep the map readable.

Lime means a physical bike facility passes your rules; a dotted-center lime line is an off-street/shared-use trail. Dashed blue marks a designated bike route, solid blue means another road passes, muted purple means caution, dark red dashes mean failure, and gray means insufficient data. The planned route uses the same symbols; failing portions pulse. Use the map legend for the exact swatches.

Limits of the data

Coverage and tagging vary by place. A missing shoulder, bike facility, traffic volume, surface, or sight-distance measurement is not proof that it is absent. Always use local signs and conditions; the app is a planning aid, not a guarantee of safety.
