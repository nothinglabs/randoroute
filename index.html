Routing settings

Limits

Minimum shoulder is the width required on faster roads. Max acceptable speed without shoulder lets slower roads pass even where shoulder data is missing. Never allow roads faster than makes roads above the selected speed fail; “no cutoff” leaves the shoulder rule in charge.

Options

Strongly prefer bike routes for all route options forces every choice to favor designated trails and corridors. Strongly prefer residential streets for all route options forces every choice to favor streets mapped as residential or living streets. Allow freeway as last resort permits a freeway only as a heavily penalized fallback; it still fails the rules. Only show fully safe routes returns no route instead of showing an unavoidable failing road. Auto-reroute waits for moving time while you remain off route, then announces and updates the route. Unknown shoulder treated as 0 ft requires faster roads to prove an adequate shoulder.

Plan a safer bike route

  1. Set Start and End by search or by tapping the map.
  2. Compare the distinct route choices, then tap a route-card item to highlight concerns.
  3. Use Settings for your riding rules, Layers to inspect the source data, and ⓘ for route details.
  4. Tap Navigate for foreground GPS and voice guidance.

Help

Map data & routing graph

The app combines official Washington data with OpenStreetMap and elevation into one offline, on-device routing graph. Layer switches change what is drawn; they do not remove data from routing.

Official WSDOT data

Bicycle Level of Traffic Stress (BLTS) supplies statewide road stress, measured shoulder context, and access-control information. Roadway Characteristic Data supplies legal speeds on state highways. Active Transportation Facilities identifies shared lanes, bike lanes, buffered or separated lanes, and shared-use paths. Permanent bicycle restrictions are hard exclusions: the router never traverses them.

OpenStreetMap data

OSM supplies the connected road and path network, street names, signed speeds where tagged, road class, one-way rules, shoulders, bicycle access, cycleways, designated national/regional bicycle routes, ferries, residential streets, and pedestrian connections. Missing speed limits use a documented road-class estimate; the street popup says when speed is estimated. A path tagged bicycle=no is never routed.

Short walk-bike fallbacks

Mapped sidewalks and crossings, paved footways, pedestrian streets, explicit bicycle=dismount connections, and road-side sidewalk tags can form a walk-only graph edge. They are priced at walking speed and shown separately—not called bike infrastructure. The router limits walk-bike use to about 0.5 mile per route so it remains a short safety fallback.

Elevation & curve cautions

AWS Terrarium elevation tiles provide climb/descent and grade-aware travel time. The builder also looks for a tight curve that overlaps an uphill grade on a faster road without a protective facility, then adjusts concern using known shoulder width. This is a possible limited-visibility uphill curve, directional and separately reported. It is a geometry/elevation proxy—not measured sight distance—and does not by itself fail your riding rules.

How routes are chosen

The router tests several time/safety and road-preference combinations, removes nearly identical paths, and presents up to five meaningfully different results. Rule failures, freeways, limited access, curve cautions, speed, grade, facilities, designated routes, and residential preference affect cost. Freeways remain a last resort; legal limited-access highways are cautions; permanent prohibitions are impassable.

What each map layer means

  • Designated routes (USBR & trails): official national/regional OSM bicycle-route relationships, drawn orange beneath the scored network.
  • WSDOT BLTS (state highways): official state-road stress and shoulder/access context.
  • OSM bike infrastructure: mapped bike lanes, cycle tracks, shared-use paths, and other bicycle facilities.
  • Bikes prohibited (WSDOT): permanent state restriction data for inspection; these graph segments are excluded from routing.
  • All roads (OSM, est. speeds): the broader street network, including residential roads; its visibility is reduced when zoomed out to keep the map readable.

Blue means comfortable or meets the selected criteria, orange means caution, red dashes mean a road fails the current rules, and gray means unknown/no data. Use the map legend for the current exact swatches.

Limits of the data

Coverage and tagging vary by place. A missing shoulder, bike facility, traffic volume, surface, or sight-distance measurement is not proof that it is absent. Always use local signs and conditions; the app is a planning aid, not a guarantee of safety.
