Washington Bike Safety

Route details

Plan a route

Use Start and End in the map bar, then tap the map or search for each place. Use + after both are set to add an optional stop.

Choose a routing style

Direct is the quickest ride. Balanced avoids bad roads when the detour is reasonable. Low-stress is willing to detour farther to avoid rule-failing roads. In Settings → Options, Always strongly prefer bike routes favors designated trails and corridors; Always strongly prefer residential streets favors streets mapped as residential or living streets over ordinary tertiary streets.

Understand the map

Tap Bike routes, Highways, Caution, or Fails rules in the route card to highlight those portions of the line. A red pulse marks a road the router could not avoid under your current rules.

Read the safety labels

These describe different things, so more than one can apply to the same stretch.

  • Comfy is the lower-stress end of the data.
  • Meets rules passes your current shoulder and speed limits.
  • Caution is a bike-legal limited-access highway that still meets your rules. It is not a rule violation, but it deserves extra judgment.
  • Fails rules has a known conflict with your selected limits, such as speed or shoulder width. The router uses it only when no reasonable alternative exists, unless you require a fully safe route.
  • Bike routes, highways, and freeways are road or route types, not safety grades. Their buttons simply show where those types occur.

Adjust your rules

In Routing Settings, set your shoulder and speed requirements. The app avoids roads that fail them unless there is no reasonable alternative.

Use navigation

After making a route, tap Navigate and allow location access. If you leave the route, the navigation banner names the planned street to return to and offers Reroute back to the nearest planned point. Auto-reroute is on by default: after 15 seconds of moving off route it announces “updating route” and reroutes from your current location.
